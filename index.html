Our Mission

To foster a community of students dedicated to the behavior, ecology, physiology, and conservation of reptiles and amphibians.

Education

Expanding our knowledge of native species, their habitats, and their ecological significance through meetings and guest lectures.

Conservation

Actively participating in local conservation efforts to protect fragile ecosystems and the reptiles and amphibians that rely on them.

Ethical Field Study

Organizing field trips ("herping") that strictly adhere to best practices, ensuring minimal impact on wildlife.

Community

Building a supportive, inclusive network of students who share a passion for nature and wildlife conservation.

How to Ethically Herp

Our primary rule in the field is respect. The animals and habitats we visit are fragile. We follow these ethical guidelines:

Leave No Trace

Pack out all trash, stay on established trails where possible, and avoid trampling sensitive vegetation.

Put It Back How You Found It

If you flip a log, rock, or tin piece to look for animals, always carefully place it back exactly as it was. It serves as a microhabitat roof!

Minimize Animal Stress

Observe without handling whenever possible. If handling is necessary for identification, do it quickly, with clean hands, and release the animal exactly where found.

Respect Wildlife Laws

Never trespass on private property. Know and follow all local, state, and federal wildlife protection laws. Collecting for personal pets is strictly prohibited on club trips.

"Take nothing but pictures, leave nothing but footprints."
